<h1 className="text-3xl font-semibold mb-6">My Linux Journey:</h1>

[← back](/n/3)
<br />

I started using Linux in the summer of 2025, when I bought an old ThinkPad from a coworker, named Timmy, during my first internship. He installed it for me by just plugging in a USB drive and following instructions. He asked me what distro I wanted, and I told him I didn't know. Debian sounded cool, and KDE Plasma sounded even cooler, so I started with that.

I riced my KDE/Debian device before I even knew what ricing meant. I loved tinkering and just putting things how I liked. It was fun running into problems and figuring out how to fix them. Consequently, I realized that if I really wanted to, I could have AI solve every problem I have. I don't think that's fun, though, so instead, I look at old forums and scavenge whatever I can find. I keep AI as a last resort.

The ThinkPad I had was way too thick, and I wanted to buy a new one that I could actually daily. I found a ThinkPad T480 on eBay for 150, and bought it. I knew I wanted to download Linux on it like my other ThinkPad, but really didn't know how to. When Timmy first installed Linux on the laptop he sold me, he really didn't teach me what anything was, he just went full speed ahead. But in my mind I was thinking "If it's really just plugging in a USB and following instructions, I can do it myself." I searched up a YouTube video, went to the Dollar General around the block, and bought a USB. I learned how to use Balena Etcher, and everything went smoothly.

I started off using Debian, because that's what I was comfortable with, but I switched to GNOME. I was back using KDE in less than 2 days. Around the same time, the company where I was working was throwing away old gaming PCs because they had recently upgraded. I asked if I could take one home and they said yes. So now I had a gaming tower laying around. I decided to try running Mint on it, since I only ever heard good things about it. It was cool, but I still switched back to KDE.


I thought that Debian and KDE was my preferred distro/desktop environment combo.

In every Linux YouTube video, any Linux subreddit, any TikTok, or Reel, I would see the same comment: "I use Arch btw." I wanted to run Arch btw too. I searched up a tutorial, went to a website to download an Arch Linux mirror, which I already thought was weird, used my baby, Balena Etcher, plugged it into the antique gaming PC, and spammed F10 and F5, because I didn't know which one it was for this machine, until I reached the BIOS menu. The menu wasn't pretty, it was just text and I couldn't even use my mouse. Everything was going too well, but then it didn't let me install it. I looked at the ArchWiki, I looked at everything, I even asked AI and nothing. The computer was running Ethernet, but pinging google.com still wasn't working. "I probably have a broken mirror," I thought. I had downloaded the MIT one, which I thought was the best, but who cares, I always liked Caltech better anyway. I tried it again and nothing. This was the first time where figuring out a problem wasn't easy.

At this point, I had recently moved into my first apartment and remembered that in order for me to connect the internet to any device, I had to go to a website, file it, and restart that device. I then tried it using the hotspot on my phone: it worked. My apartment's wifi provider absolutely HATES Linux or any type of local network. I could finally use Arch!! I downloaded Hyprland because I wanted to be aesthetic, and away I went.

I got into a desktop environment and I couldn't access anything, the top bar said something about the config file. I researched what that even was and I went into the config file. I love how I can absolutely map everything. I didn't love how nothing was installed. Well, I appreciated there was no bloatware, but I was just being lazy.


I made the following additions to my laptop:


- installed the GNOME settings after not liking any of the ones that Reddit suggested I use for Hyprland


- got Hyprland settings to change the transparency and do other cool modifications to my windows


- added a bar on the top of my screen for the time, Bluetooth and wifi status, and other essentials


- got cool wallpapers


- installed Libre Office


- got a app launcher to search up apps instead of having them run in the terminal


- installed an autocorrect


- installed Spotify, Visual Studio Code, and Firefox


- etc.


It is my main device. I have a Dell XPS 13 with Windows that I use for assignments that might need Lockdown Browser, Adobe products, and Autodesk products. I carry both like the true larper I am in my backpack, but mostly always use the ThinkPad.
